# M24: the cache of the resolver side, a 1 M-name mixed run and an upstream cut (Windows)

Verdict: PASS (documented: the cache held its bound of 100,000 entries, 99,968 held, through 25 M queries over a population of 661,730 questions; hit ratio 0.837 at that bound and 1.000 once every question fits, 0.96 over the warm-up; p99 3.5 ms closed loop and 1.2 ms paced at the bound, 0.8 ms and 1.2 ms when everything is cached; while the upstream was cut for 30 s, 596,878 stale answers and no error or timeout among 1.2 M queries)

M24 of the Phase 2 plan (section 6) asks for the hit ratio and the p99 of a 1 M-name mixed run and for serve-stale answers under an upstream cut, documented; WP5.1 adds that the cache holds its bound. This run measures `tenko serve --roles controller,dns` (the forwarder and cache of WP5.1, commit `66a8a13`) with its default forwarder pointed at the engine's example server (`crates/tenko-dns/examples/authoritative.rs`) serving the 1,000,000-name `zonegen` zone, and the `bench/phase2-dns` load generator asking with RD set, all on one host over loopback, with `scripts/run-m24-cache.sh`. Every figure is a single-node figure (plan section 2.10) and a loopback figure: an upper bound, with the resolver, the upstream and the load generator sharing the cores.

## The query mix

`zonegen --records 1000000 --queries 1000000` writes 1,000,000 queries sampled with replacement from the 1,000,000 names (5 percent NXDOMAIN); the mixed file adds the first 10,000 of them 400 times, so that 80 percent of the queries ask a hot set of 10,000 questions and 20 percent ask the whole population. The file has 5,000,000 lines and 661,730 distinct questions; the load generator picks a line at random per query. NXDOMAIN answers are cached as negative entries (RFC 2308) and counted as valid answers by the load generator.

## The cache at its bound

Resolver: 2 UDP workers, the default forwarder at the upstream (`forward_timeout_ms = 1000`, `forward_retries = 1`), `[dns.cache] entries` 100,000 or 1,000,000 with `max_bytes` raised to 8 GiB so that the entry bound is the one that binds, prefetch on, serve-stale on. Load: 4 sockets with 32 queries in flight each (closed loop), or paced at 20,000 queries per second. The cache counters come from the resolver's `/metrics` before and after each pass; CPU and RSS from the load generator's sampling of the resolver process. The warm pass is 60 s, the measured passes 30 s each, run on the warm cache in this order.

No pass had a timeout or an error. Reading it:

- **The bound holds.** With 100,000 entries allowed and 661,730 distinct questions asked, the cache held 99,968 entries from the warm-up on (the 64 shards hold 1,562 each; the least recently used entry of a shard goes when a new one comes). The 1,000,000-entry cache held 645,101 entries, one per distinct question the run had asked by then, so that bound was never reached; the octet bound of 8 GiB was not either.
- **Hit ratio.** At the 100,000 bound the hot set stays and the cold fifth mostly misses: 0.837, steady from the warm-up on. With room for every question the ratio reaches 1.000 once each has been asked (0.960 over the warm-up, which asked the cold names for the first time).
- **Latency.** The closed loop at the bound has a p99 of 3.5 ms: a sixth of the queries go upstream, and their round trip through the forwarder, the upstream and back sets the tail. When everything is cached, p99 is 0.8 ms closed and 1.2 ms paced. The closed loop is limited by the load generator and the three processes on the same cores (about 266,000 QPS in every pass), not by the cache: the CPU per query falls from 14.7 us with a sixth of the queries forwarded to 7.4 us with none, and to 5 us paced.
- **Memory.** The resolver's RSS was 62 to 70 MiB with about 10,000 entries (the serve-stale run below), 126 MiB at the 100,000 bound and 289 MiB with 645,101 entries. Taking about 60 MiB as the process without a cache, that is about 650 octets per entry at the 100,000 bound, where entries are evicted and replaced all the time, and about 360 with 645,101 entries that only grew, for answers of one A record whose stored form (without the OPT record) is 50 to 60 octets, plus the key, the slot and the map entry (section 3.1 of `docs/dns-resolver.md`). A 100,000-entry cache, the default outside the Home profile, costs about 65 MiB; the Home default of 10,000 about 6 MiB.

## Serve-stale under an upstream cut

Resolver: entries live 5 seconds at most (`max_ttl_secs = 5`), a stale answer after 400 ms (`stale_answer_ms = 400`), the stale window of one day, 100,000 entries, prefetch on. Load: the hot set of 10,000 questions. After a 10 s closed-loop warm-up, a paced run of 60 s at 20,000 queries per second; from its 10th second the upstream process is killed, and it is started again 30 s later (on Windows there is no SIGSTOP: a killed upstream refuses the queries at once, where a stopped one lets them time out; the Linux path of the script stops it). Then 10 s more at the same rate.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| warm, closed loop 10 s | 268,143 | 448.5 | 913.9 | 3,850.2 | 10,354.7 | 0 | 0 | 2,658,531 | 23,026 | 0 |
| paced 60 s, upstream cut from 10 s to 40 s | 19,996 | 461.1 | 1,706.0 | 4,419.6 | 9,257.0 | 0 | 0 | 549,647 | 650,094 | 596,878 |
| paced 10 s after the upstream came back | 19,971 | 438.8 | 1,696.8 | 5,140.5 | 9,265.2 | 0 | 0 | 180,534 | 19,178 | 0 |

Every one of the 1,199,741 queries of the cut pass was answered, with no timeout and no error. While the upstream was gone, the entries expired within 5 s and every query for an expired entry was answered from it with the stale TTL of 30 s: 596,878 stale answers. A killed upstream refuses at once and the pool puts it into back-off after three failures, so the forwarder answered stale without waiting for the 400 ms budget (the p99 of the pass is 1.7 ms); a stopped upstream (the Linux path) would cost the budget until the back-off. When the upstream came back, the misses went upstream again and refreshed the entries: no stale answer in the last pass, and the hit ratio back to 0.90 (the entries live 5 s, so a tenth of the queries refresh them).

## Not measured here

- **Linux and the musl binary.** The first attempt ran in WSL2 with the static musl binary, the binary that ships. On this host WSL2 runs with the VirtioProxy network mode, whose loopback relay sends traffic for 127.0.0.1 to Windows and swallows the first packets to a newly bound port on the other loopback addresses: the upstream exchanges of the forwarder were lost or delayed by a second there, a property of the host, not of the resolver (the run found and fixed two real issues on the way: queries to a loopback upstream now leave from its address, and sockets are bound off the runtime threads). The Linux figure needs a Linux host or a VM without that relay (plan M17's VMs); the script's Linux path (SIGSTOP and SIGCONT) is written but was not run to the end.
- **DoT and DoH upstreams under load**: this run asks the upstream over plain UDP. The three transports are covered for function by the compose lab (`deploy/compose/lab-dns`) and the tests (`crates/tenko-dns/tests/upstreams.rs`), not for throughput.
- **Prefetch** is not exercised: the zonegen TTLs (300 to 3,600 s) do not run out within the passes, and entries that live under 10 s, those of the serve-stale run, are never prefetched (23,026 misses in its 10 s warm-up: each of the 10,000 questions expired about twice). Its function is covered by the unit tests of `crates/tenko-dns/src/cache`.
