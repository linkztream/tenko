# M42: the denial cache's effect (the compose lab, Linux)

Verdict: PASS (with the denial cache, 1 to 5 queries reached the authority per 1,000 random names under one covered range, NSEC and NSEC3 alike, under every QNAME minimisation mode; without it 1,001 to 1,005, one per name; the threshold is below 50 with the cache and 1,000 without)

M42 of the recursion plan (section 6) counts the queries the recursive resolver sends to the authority for 1,000 random names under a range one NSEC record covers, and the same in the NSEC3 zone (zero extra iterations), with the denial cache of RFC 8198 on and off ([dns-resolver.md](../../../docs/dns-resolver.md) section 11.2). The brief of WP10.3 adds the three modes of QNAME minimisation (relaxed, strict, off). Every figure is a single-node figure: one Tenko node under test against the lab's own root.

## Procedure

`scripts/run-m42-denial.sh` runs the round `recursion` of the compose DNS lab (`deploy/compose/lab-dns/smoke.sh` with `ROUNDS=recursion M42=1`; the measurement is `denial_m42` in `denial.sh`):

- The node under test is the lab image (`deploy/Dockerfile`: the static musl release binary that ships, `tenko 0.0.3 (root anchors 20326, 38696; root hints 2026-10-21)`, built from the WP10.3 tree), resolving from the lab root with validation on and the lab root's DS as its trust anchor. The authority is a second Tenko node from the same image serving the root, the TLD `test`, `example.test` (NSEC) and `nsec3.example.test` (NSEC3, no salt, no extra iterations), signed online.
- For each of the six settings (`aggressive_nsec` true or false, `qname_minimisation` relaxed, strict or off) the node is restarted with them (its caches empty, its data kept).
- One NXDOMAIN first, whose proof covers the range: `nothing.example.test` (its NSEC runs from `loop2.example.test` to `nsec3.example.test`) and `a.nsec3.example.test` (the zone's two NSEC3 records cover every hash but the apex's and `www`'s).
- Then 1,000 names `m` plus 12 random letters and digits under the zone (all in that range), one after the other with `dig -f` from the client container.
- The count is the authority's query log: the lines for names at or below the zone from the start of the batch, every page read. It includes the queries of the validator (DS and DNSKEY) and of minimisation, so it is everything the authority saw for the zone.

Host: the WSL2 Ubuntu 24.04 distribution of the development machine (kernel 6.18, 32 threads, 50 GiB), Docker 29.1.3, the lab's bridge network; 2026-10-23.

## Figures

Authority queries per 1,000 names, runs 2 and 3 (the engine that remembers the names a minimised question showed to exist; run 3 is the final tree):

| Zone | Minimisation | Denial cache on, run 2 | Run 3 | Denial cache off, run 2 | Run 3 |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| NSEC | relaxed | 1 | 5 | 1,001 | 1,001 |
| NSEC3 | relaxed | 4 | 4 | 1,004 | 1,004 |
| NSEC | strict | 1 | 1 | 1,001 | 1,005 |
| NSEC3 | strict | 4 | 4 | 1,004 | 1,004 |
| NSEC | off | 4 | 1 | 1,001 | 1,004 |
| NSEC3 | off | 3 | 3 | 1,003 | 1,003 |

With the cache, the few queries are the validator's and the resolution of names the batch does not cover: the first query of the batch can come before the key sets of the zone are in place after the restart (DS and DNSKEY), and the counts move by a handful between runs for that reason. Without it, every name costs the one query of its NXDOMAIN, plus the same handful.

## The first run, before the engine remembered existing names

Run 1 (the first WP10.3 commit) gave the same figures with the cache (2 to 6) but, without it, 2,002 and 2,004 under relaxed and strict minimisation against 1,004 and 1,003 with minimisation off: every name cost two queries. The lab's authority serves the root, the TLD and the zones under it from one address and answers from the deepest zone it holds, without a referral (as BIND does for a parent and a child it both serves), so the resolver never learns the cut of `example.test` and every walk minimised through `example.test` again. The engine now remembers a name a minimised question showed to exist without a cut for the infrastructure TTL ([dns-resolver.md](../../../docs/dns-resolver.md) section 11.1), and runs 2 and 3 cost the one query per name that minimisation off costs.

## Not measured here

- **The load VM and the node VM.** The plan's measurements of section 6 run the authority on the load VM and the node on the node VM; M42 counts queries, which do not depend on where the processes run, so the lab on one host gives the same figure. M36 (resolutions per second) is WP10.5's.
- **A flood of random names**: the run is sequential, one name after another. The figure per 1,000 names is what a random-subdomain flood under a signed zone costs the authority with and without the cache (risk R84).
