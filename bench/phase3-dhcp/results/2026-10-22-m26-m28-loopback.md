# M26 to M28 on loopback: the WP7.3 engine with its own load client (Windows)

Verdict: DOCUMENTED (own client, loopback, Windows). With the commit hand-off and no store behind it, the engine answers about 86,000 to 107,000 4-way exchanges per second closed loop, 57,000 to 97,000 renewals per second, and at 1,000 exchanges per second a p99 from DISCOVER to ACK of 0.24 to 0.27 ms; with `MemLeaseStore`, the contracts' in-memory reference, about 5,500 exchanges per second at 10,000 leases and a p99 of 17 ms at 1,000 per second. None of these is the fallback figure of P3-Q1: that needs the store of WP7.2 under `tenko serve` and perfdhcp on the musl binary (WP7.6).

M26 asks for the 4-way exchanges per second with every lease committed before the ACK, M27 for the renewals per second after a fill, M28 for the p99 from DISCOVER to ACK and the commit's share of it (plan section 6). This run measures the engine of WP7.3 through its example server (`crates/tenko-dhcp/examples/dhcp_server.rs`: the relay listener, the workers, the committer, the hashed allocator, the offer table and a hash lease table) with the load client of the same example, because perfdhcp is a Linux tool and WSL could not be used from this session. The figures are labelled "own client, loopback, Windows"; the perfdhcp rows are WP7.6's. Every figure is a single-node figure (plan section 2.12) and a loopback figure: an upper bound.

## Setup

- **Server.** `dhcp_server serve --listen 127.0.0.1:6767 --relay-port 6868 --workers N --store S --idle-exit 3`: one relay-mode listener (one shared socket on Windows), N workers, one scope `198.18.0.0/15` (RFC 2544's benchmarking range) with a pool of 131,053 addresses and a fixed lease time of 3,600 s, three options. Every ACK leaves only after its lease change has gone through `LeaseStore::submit`, come back as a committed completion and been applied to the lease table.
- **Stores.** `queue`: the `LeaseStore` contract with no rows behind it (the hand-off into a bounded queue, the committer's drain and the completion), which isolates the engine, the workers and the commit path of the server. `mem`: `MemLeaseStore`, whose commit keeps its client index by walking the scope's rows and copying each client identity, so its cost grows with the leases; it ran with 10,000 clients. Neither is the production store (WP7.2).
- **Load.** `dhcp_server load --server 127.0.0.1:6767 --bind 127.0.0.1:6868`: a relay agent (`giaddr` 127.0.0.1, option 118 for the scope, option 61 per client), one sender thread and one receiver thread that answers each OFFER with the REQUEST. Closed loop keeps 64 (run 1) or 256 (run 2) exchanges in flight; open loop paces new exchanges at the rate. An exchange without its ACK after 2 s counts as lost. The latency is from the first message of the exchange (DISCOVER, or the renewal's REQUEST) to the ACK, measured by the client.
- **Script.** `bench/phase3-dhcp/scripts/run-loopback.sh` (one fresh server per row; raw logs under `results/raw/`, not committed).

## Reading

- **The commit is the budget, as the plan says.** With the queue store the server's own times stay at the first bucket (50 µs) for the OFFER and the ACK at 1,000 and 5,000 exchanges per second; the client-side p99 of 0.22 to 0.27 ms is the two round trips over loopback, two worker passes and one committer pass. With `MemLeaseStore` the same path at 1,000 per second has a p99 of 17 ms, and all of it is the commit (the hand-off-to-commit histogram matches the ACK's): its commit walks the scope's rows and copies an identity per row, so its cost grows with the table. That is the reference implementation doing what an oracle may; it says nothing about the coalescer path of WP7.2, which M21's 15 to 19 ms p99 at 1,000 changes per second (Phase 2) predicts.
- **The closed-loop ceiling is not the workers'.** 86,000 exchanges per second with one worker and 92,000 to 107,000 with two to eight, at either window: adding workers did not raise it. Two single threads bound it: the load client's receiver, which parses every OFFER and ACK and sends every REQUEST, and the server's committer, which applies each committed lease to the table under its write lock and sends each ACK. The renewal rows show the committer: at window 256 the hand-off-to-commit time grows to a p50 of 2.5 ms, the time ACKs wait for it, while the workers' OFFER times stay at 50 µs. At 8 workers with window 256 the rate fell to 47,501 per second: eight readers of the table's lock against the committer's writes, and one shared socket on Windows (the layout of ADR 0009 there). Sharding the committer and the table's lock is the next step if a store ever commits faster than this; WP7.2's store will not, by the measured commit rates of Phase 2 (M5: about 3,000 changes per second closed loop with immediate durability), so the engine is not where M26's fallback line will be decided.
- **`MemLeaseStore` above its rate.** At 5,000 exchanges per second over 100,000 clients its commit time grew past the client's 2 s timeout as the rows grew (p99 1.6 s), 86,640 exchanges were lost and the server's committed leases stopped at 16,894; nothing was dropped by the server, the ACKs simply came too late. The row is kept to show why the reference store runs with 10,000 clients.
- **The engine's CPU per exchange** was not sampled in this run (the generator shared the host); the server's step times of at most 50 µs at the median and the 86,000 exchanges per second of one worker, two datagrams in and two out per exchange, bound the one worker's share from above at about 12 µs per exchange (1 s over 86,040), the committer's pass not included.

## Caveats

- **Own client, loopback, Windows.** perfdhcp did not run (Linux only, and WSL could not be used from this session); the load client is the example's, one process with two threads on the same host. The musl figures of the plan's measurement rule are WP7.6's, with perfdhcp and the store; these rows are not the M26 and M28 verdicts.
- **Short closed-loop rows.** 100,000 clients finish in about one second at these rates, so the M26 rows are bursts of one second, not a sustained minute; the M27 and M28 rows ran 20 s.
- **Single runs per configuration** and other work on the host (another session's builds ran at times); run 1 and run 2 agree within 5 percent where the window does not change the row.
- **Not the shipped binary.** The example links the engine as `tenko serve` will, but it is not `tenko`; the role's wiring is WP7.5.
