# ADR 0025: DHCP failover and data-plane high availability

- Status: Accepted (the owner decided HA-Q1 to HA-Q15 of the plan on 2026-10-25)
- Date: 2026-10-25

## Context

The owner compared Tenko with Infoblox, BlueCat and EfficientIP and decided that high availability of the data plane, above all DHCP failover, is one of two gaps that keep Tenko out of production and is to be done right from the start. The plan is [ha-plan.md](../plans/ha-plan.md); this ADR records its design.

What exists. ADR 0002 point 4 and DHCP-07 kept leases out of the global Raft group and on a local log per node, synchronised between failover partners. The Phase 3 plan's decision P3-Q1 and ADR 0019 point 3 (Proposed) changed that: a lease is a row of the replicated store, committed before the ACK, with an asynchronous publication model (a local durable log first, publication to the store after the answer) as the measured fallback of ADR 0019 point 4. P3-Q2 moved failover pairs and their chaos test (DHCP-12, DHCP-18) to Phase 5 and had Phase 3 build what a pair would need: the failover draft's fields on the lease row (`draft-ietf-dhc-failover-12`), the `PoolOwnership` trait, `grant_time` with a partner limit, the `LeaseStore` trait and the lease node commands. One node was built and measured: about 10,000 4-way exchanges per second with a p99 of 19 ms from REQUEST to ACK at 1,000 per second, the commit to the store being almost all of the time, and no acknowledged lease lost across kills (M26 to M28, M32). A `dns` or `dhcp` node without a controller in its process does not exist yet; node sync feeds only probes.

What constrains the design:

- CONTRIBUTING rule 5 and OPS-02: no controller round trip in the DORA exchange or the renew path, and the data plane keeps serving with every controller down. On a cluster of more than one voter a commit to the replicated store waits for a majority, so the lease path of ADR 0019 point 3 would break the rule as soon as the `dhcp` role ran on a controller of a cluster, and a quorum loss would stop every new lease (ADR 0019 says so in its consequences).
- Two machines must be enough. A Raft group of two voters has no fault tolerance, so at home one machine is a controller and the other a node that is not a voter; whatever the pair needs while one machine is gone cannot come from a quorum.
- No address may be acknowledged to two clients at overlapping times, and no acknowledged lease may be lost (DHCP-18). Nothing is merged silently (OPS-26).
- DHCPv4 failover has no RFC; the Windows and ISC variants are not interoperable (CON-05). ISC DHCP reached end of life in 2022. RFC 8156 standardises DHCPv6 failover, but neither Kea nor ISC dhcpd nor Windows implements it; Kea's HA hook takes the failover protocol's concepts and uses its own protocol, with the lease update sent to the partner before the answer.

Three designs were compared (plan section 2): a failover pair after the draft; the replicated store as the only arbiter, with two nodes serving one scope behind conditional writes; and a hybrid that uses the store while it has a quorum and the draft's behaviour when it has not. The second breaks rule 5 and fails with two machines; the third builds both and runs its safety-critical path only during failures.

This ADR supersedes point 3 of ADR 0019 for where a lease lives (ADR 0019 is Proposed; its status line names the change) and reverses P3-Q2's placement of failover in Phase 5, as the owner decided in HA-Q1 on 2026-10-25. ADR 0002 point 4 stays in force.

## Decision

1. **The lease belongs to the node that serves the scope.** Every node with the `dhcp` role has a node store: a one-voter instance of `tenko-store` of its own, never a member of the controllers' Raft group. A lease change is committed to it before the ACK by the committer that exists today. A scope is served by exactly one node or one pair; two nodes never serve one scope outside a pair.
2. **The controllers hold the published copy.** Each node publishes its committed lease and history rows to the controllers' store after the answer, in batches, from a cursor over its journal, idempotently; the controllers merge rows by a per-binding version, write the IPAM observation fact in the same batch and keep the fields they own (the DDNS outcome). The reconciler, DDNS, the API, the UI, the Kea export, the history retention and the backup read the published copy as before. While no controller can write, publications wait on the node. ADR 0019 point 4's fallback is thereby the production path, for every deployment including a lone node.
3. **Failover is a pair after the draft's model, not its wire format.** Two nodes form a pair for a set of scopes, in load balance (clients split by the RFC 3074 hash of the client identifier, else `chaddr`, 50/50 by default) or hot standby (a 5 percent reserve for the standby by default). A node may be in several pairs. The binding states are the draft's, already on the row; the server states are startup, normal, communications-interrupted, partner-down, recover, recover-wait, recover-done, potential-conflict, paused and shutdown, persisted in the node store.
4. **Addresses are split by a fixed function.** Each pool address belongs to one partner by its hash into 256 buckets under the pair's split; both nodes compute it, no message moves it, and resizing a pool moves nothing. `PoolOwnership` answers both "may I offer this address" and "do I answer this client" from the split and the node's state. Dynamic rebalancing is not built; the row's `share` field is reserved for it.
5. **The MCLT bounds every lease.** A grant ends no later than the partner's acknowledged potential end plus the MCLT (default one hour), in every state, through `grant_time`. Updates are lazy, except that a new binding waits up to 50 ms in the normal state for the partner's acknowledgement, so it gets the full lease time and lies on two disks when the partner is quick; renewals never wait.
6. **Communications-interrupted serves everyone from the own share; partner-down needs evidence.** Without contact a node answers every client, allocating only from its own share, so two partners cut off from each other never give one address twice and a survivor keeps every client served without an operator. Partner-down, which lets the survivor take the partner's share after the MCLT, is entered by an administrator with step-up authentication or automatically after a safe period (600 s by default) only on evidence that the partner is not serving: a quorate controller reports no heartbeat from it, or clients of the partner's share wait unanswered past a delay. The profiles set the automatic rule.
7. **Recovery and conflicts.** A returning node with its store intact catches up from the partner's journal and returns to normal; an empty one takes every binding and holds new allocations for the MCLT. When both may have allocated from one share without contact, the pair resolves every address held by two clients in potential-conflict, the later client transaction time keeping the address, and counts, shows and audits each conflict.
8. **Lease sync runs over the cluster port's mutual TLS.** A `Failover` service carries the binding updates in the sender's revision order; the receiver commits before it acknowledges; a version per binding orders the updates of one address; resync reads the journal from a cursor, or every binding when the journal no longer covers it. The service accepts only the partner the pair names, by its node certificate. There is no shared secret: the vault purpose `dhcp.failover` is withdrawn, and IAM-16 and NEVER-01 lose "failover secrets".
9. **DHCPv6 uses RFC 8156's model on the same channel.** Independent allocation for IA_NA (the survivor never takes the partner's share), a fixed split for IA_PD, the same states and MCLT on valid lifetimes, the same hash over the DUID for load balance. RFC 8156's wire protocol is not implemented.
10. **DDNS stays one leader service over the published copy.** Both partners derive the same DHCID, so either may replace or remove the other's names. Zones change only through the store; while no controller has a quorum, DDNS and RFC 2136 updates wait, and nothing is merged afterwards.
11. **`dns` and `dhcp` nodes are fed by node sync into their node store.** Node sync consumes the `dns` and `dhcp` subsets with paged snapshots, so the existing feeds run over a node's own store and `tenko serve` runs these roles without a controller. Secrets of a role (TSIG keys) are fetched while a controller is reachable and held in memory only. Data-plane nodes are not Raft learners.
12. **Client failover for DNS is the operator's layer, driven by Tenko's health signal.** Option 6 with two addresses is the default; `tenko node health --role <role>` and a readiness route on the metrics listener drive keepalived (VRRP) or a routing daemon (anycast) the operator runs. Tenko implements neither VRRP nor BGP.
13. **Split scopes for coexistence.** A scope serving beside Windows DHCP excludes the other server's range, is non-authoritative and may delay its offers; a Windows failover relationship imported by MIG-08 becomes a Tenko pair without its secret.

## Consequences

- The data plane needs no quorum for any DHCP function: a lone node and a pair grant new leases with every controller down, which OPS-02 and OPS-26 asked for and P3-Q14's refusal path could not give. The refusal path stays for a failing node store.
- A lease is written twice: to the node store before the ACK and to the published copy after it. The single-node rate must stay within 10 percent of M26 and M28 (M62); if it does not, an unpaired scope on a controller node keeps the current path (plan HA-Q2).
- Leases live in two places. The node store is the authority and the published copy can lag; the lag is measured, shown as a finding, and the node's own view is reachable through the node command. Backup and restore of the controllers cover the published copy; a node rebuilt from nothing recovers from its partner, or, unpaired, from the published copy.
- `dhcp.expiry` becomes a node service; the observation hook moves from the committer to the publication; the lease table follows the node store. The engine, the codec, the allocator's walk, the offer hold, `LeaseStore`, the committer, DDNS and the UI are unchanged.
- The first lease of a new client is the MCLT when the partner does not acknowledge within 50 ms; clients renew every half MCLT while the partner is gone.
- Data-plane nodes open the cluster port to their partner (8444/TCP between partners).
- The pair's correctness rests on a state machine whose rare paths are tested by a simulator with random schedules, by real-process tests through the cluster harness's relays and by the chaos lab; the plan's E60 to E71 are the evidence for acceptance.
- Interoperability with other servers' failover protocols is given up for good, as CON-05 already said; coexistence is split scopes.
- Sites that must keep changing DNS while cut off still need OPS-27.

## Alternatives considered

- **The replicated store as the only arbiter** (plan section 2.3): one durability story and any number of servers per scope, but a Raft commit in every DORA exchange on a cluster (against rule 5), no new leases without a quorum, and no two-machine story. Serving through a quorum loss would need a local log, an ownership split and a merge, which is this decision inside the other one.
- **A hybrid of the store and the draft** (plan section 2.4): builds both, runs the duplicate-preventing path only during failures, and at home runs on that path whenever one box is down.
- **Kea's model: the partner's acknowledgement before every answer.** The strongest durability, at the price of the partner's round trip on every renewal and of answers that stop until partner-down is declared; kept only for new bindings, with a 50 ms bound (decision point 5).
- **The draft's or RFC 8156's wire protocol on TCP 647 with a shared secret.** No peer implements RFC 8156, ISC DHCP is at end of life and Windows is not interoperable; a second transport and a secret would add nothing the cluster port's mutual TLS lacks.
- **The draft's dynamic pool balancing (POOLREQ).** Adapts to uneven demand at the cost of state per free address; a fixed split drains evenly under load balance and is kept until a measurement asks for more.
- **Partner-down by hand only, or automatically on a timer alone.** The first leaves a home on half a pool until someone acts; the second makes both sides of a partition take over. Evidence plus a timer, with the hand always available, is the middle.
- **Data-plane nodes as Raft learners.** The least code, but every learner would receive every table, far beyond its role, and the leader would replicate every write to every node.
- **VRRP or BGP inside Tenko.** Needs privileges the data plane does not hold under ADR 0023 and duplicates mature daemons.
- **A third, witness voter so that two DHCP controllers keep a quorum.** Contradicts the two-machine requirement and still puts a quorum on the DORA path.
