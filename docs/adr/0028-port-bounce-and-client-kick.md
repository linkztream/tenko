# ADR 0028: Bouncing a port and kicking a client after a move

- Status: Accepted (the owner decided the design on 2026-10-26; the choices of this record within it are the builder's)
- Date: 2026-10-26
- Extends: [ADR 0027](0027-moving-clients-and-the-short-lease.md), whose alternatives left "forcing the client off its port" to this part

## Context

A move job ([moves.md](../moves.md)) changes a device's network through the network controller and then waits until the device is seen at its new address. A device that does not notice the change waits for its short lease (ADR 0027): about two minutes. Two writes on the controller make it start over at once: an administrative down and up of its switch port, and a disconnect of a wireless client from its access point. Both are writes with side effects on whatever else sits behind the port, and a port left down cuts its device off, so the owner decided on 2026-10-26 that they come with their own guardrails and that the port's way back up is owned by the connector host, never by the job (CONTRIBUTING: security first).

## Decision

1. **Two optional actions in the connector protocol.** Three new members of `L2Change` (`tenko.connect.v1`, field numbers 6 to 8, additive within version 1): `BouncePort` (device, port, `down_secs` 1 to 30) is the down half of a bounce; `EnablePort` (device, port, an opaque `restore` of at most 16 KiB) brings the port up as it was and is only ever the undo of a `BouncePort`; `KickClient` (MAC address) disconnects a wireless client. The capabilities `port_bounce` and `client_kick` declare them in the manifest and need `network_controller`. The SDK answers the changes of an undeclared capability with `UNIMPLEMENTED`; the conformance suite plans each declared one and checks that an undeclared one is refused.
2. **The plan carries the way back up.** `L2Plan` gains `undo` (field 6): a `BouncePort` plan carries exactly one `EnablePort` of the same port, so that the host can bring the port up even if it never received the receipt. Every other change leaves it empty.
3. **The re-enable belongs to the host, and a record comes first.** `ConnectorHost::bounce_port` checks the write switch, plans against a fresh topology with the guardrails, and writes a pending record to the new table `connect.bounces` (key: connector, device, port; value `PendingBounce`: the plan's diff and its way back up, when the port is due up, the job, the attempts and why the last one failed) together with the audit record `connect.port.bounce`, before the down is sent. A task of the host sends the down, waits `down_secs` and sends the up, whatever happens to the caller. The up is planned, written and read back like any write, and the record is removed with the audit record `connect.port.enable` only when the read-back shows the port up. An up that fails marks the record stuck: the health view shows `connect.port_stuck` with the connector, the device and the port, and the host tries again after 5 s, 15 s, 60 s and then every 5 minutes. At every start the host brings up every port with a record; every second it brings up the records past due that no task of its own handles (another controller that stopped, a stuck one). The up ignores the write switch, the plan age and the guardrails, because it only restores what Tenko changed seconds before.
4. **The guardrails of the two.** A bounce never touches a port the fresh topology does not have, an uplink or a port that links to another device (an access point, a switch, an LLDP neighbour), the port through which Tenko or the controller is reached or to which the controller or Tenko's host is attached, a protected port or the port of a protected client, a port with more than one client behind it (two listed clients or more than one learned MAC address: a dock, an unmanaged switch, a phone with a PC port), or a port that is down already. A kick never touches a client the topology does not have, the controller or Tenko's host, a protected client, or a client that is not wireless. The codes are `connect.guardrail.*` (new: `shared_port`, `port_down`, `port_unknown`, `client_unknown`, `not_wireless`). `DevicePort` gains `enabled` and `learned_macs` (fields 8 and 9) so that the plugin can report what the rules need.
5. **The move job.** After `ipam.move.controller`, the new automatic step `ipam.move.nudge` waits `[dhcp.moves] reappear_secs` (30) for the device to come back by itself (the dhcp role saw its exchange on the target scope, or the lease table holds its lease there), and only then bounces (after a port profile change, for `bounce_down_secs`, 5) or kicks (after a per-client VLAN), once. A refusal or a controller error then is no failure of the move; a stuck port fails the step with `connect.port_stuck` and the job rolls back. The compensation of `ipam.move.controller` does the same bounce or kick right after the L2 undo. The preview shows per device which of the two will run, or why neither (`ipam.move.nudge.no_capability`, `.refused` with the rule, `.off`), and a move can switch it off (`MoveRequest::nudge`, on by default; the preview offers the switch when a controller offers either).
6. **UniFi through the classic API.** The Integration API offers neither an administrative down nor a client disconnect (its one port action is a PoE power cycle, which Tenko never sends: it reboots the powered device). The bounce sets `forward: disabled` in the port's override entry and touches no other member; its way back up writes the previous `forward` back, or removes the entry when the bounce added it. A Network Application of release 7.4 and later configures every port inline that way (the recorded 10.6 controller of `crates/tenko-connect-unifi/tests/fixtures/recorded` has no "Disabled" profile and carries `forward` per override). Restoring one member re-enables reliably: it does not depend on a profile that exists only on some releases, and it keeps a change an administrator made to another member meanwhile. Whether a port is off is read from the configuration (the override, or a profile that forwards nothing), which the controller answers at once; a read of the port table could lag the switch for seconds and fail the read-back. The kick is the station manager's `kick-sta` (`POST cmd/stamgr`), never `block-sta`.

## Why ADR 0028 and not a section of ADR 0027

The pending record is a new table in the replicated store and a new contract between the host and every plugin (the plan's `undo`, the host-owned up). That is a model change of its own; ADR 0027 is accepted and keeps its decision.

## Consequences

- A moved device that does not react to the VLAN change is back within the reappear wait plus the bounce, about 35 s, instead of its short lease; a rollback brings it back as fast.
- A port is down for at most `down_secs` in the normal case. A controller that fails the up leaves the port down until it answers again; the health view names the port, and a person can switch it on at the controller. Deleting a connector instance drops its pending records, named in the audit record and the log.
- A bounce or a kick costs one write slot of `max_parallel` and the calls of a plan and a write; the up two plans and a write.
- The generic L2 calls (`plan_l2`, `apply_l2`, `revert_l2`) refuse the new changes with `connect.nudge.direct`: only the host's own calls own the way back up.
- In a cluster every controller sweeps the records; two controllers that bring the same port up at once are harmless (the up is idempotent and the record is removed with a revision precondition).
- A plugin in another language that declares `port_bounce` must put the `EnablePort` into the plan's `undo`, must never bring the port up by itself, and must read the port's state back from its configuration.

## Alternatives considered

- **The plugin bounces in one call (down, wait, up).** A plugin that crashes in between leaves the port down with nobody knowing; the host could not tell. Refused for security.
- **The job brings the port up.** A job that is cancelled, times out or loses its process between the two leaves the port down. Refused by the owner's design.
- **UniFi's "Disabled" port profile.** Present on older releases, absent from current ones; restoring the old profile also fails for a port without one on record.
- **A PoE power cycle.** Reboots the powered device; out of scope by decision.
- **No wait before the nudge.** Many switches drop the link on a profile change already, and the device asks at once; a bounce then is a second interruption for nothing.
