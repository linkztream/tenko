# ADR 0034: The flip of a zone in place and the adoption of mirrored leases and names

- Status: Proposed (the owner decided the design on 2026-11-01 and 2026-11-05, WT-Q3, WT-Q4, WT-Q8, WT-Q9, WT-Q10 and WT-Q12 of `docs/plans/windows-takeover-plan.md`; Accepted when WP14.2 has built the flip and the takeover and E75 and E76 have passed)
- Date: 2026-11-05

## Context

In the Windows takeover ([windows-takeover-plan.md](../plans/windows-takeover-plan.md), Slice 14) Tenko is a secondary of every AD zone until the operator flips a zone, and the DHCP data arrives by a mirrored source ([ADR 0033](0033-mirrored-sources.md)). Two things today's code does not do stand between the secondary and a served domain:

- [dns.md](../dns.md) section 1 says a zone's kind does not change: a secondary cannot become a primary. Today's runbook copies the zone once by transfer, exports it and imports it as `manual` records, which loses the provenance of every record and cannot be repeated.
- The names Windows DHCP registered carry a DHCID record (RFC 4701). Tenko's DDNS planner treats a name as a lease's own only when its DHCID RRset has the origin `dhcp` and the bytes Tenko computes, and refuses a `transfer`, `dynamic`, `manual` or `ad` name whatever `conflict` says ([ADR 0020](0020-ddns-from-dhcp.md)). Today's runbook therefore deletes every record Windows DHCP wrote before the switch (step 10), and every client is without a name until it renews.

The engine, for its part, keys a lease by option 61 when the client sends one, else by `htype` and `chaddr`, and Windows shows a client's identifier as six octets for both a client that sends option 61 of type 1 with its MAC address and one that sends none; the mirror cannot always tell which key the client will present.

## Decision

### The flip

1. **A zone flips in place.** `POST /dns/zones/{id}/flip` with `{"to": "primary", "soa": {...}, "ns": [...]}` turns a secondary zone into a primary zone: the kind changes, the transfer service stops refreshing it, the SOA `mname` and `rname` and the apex NS set become the operator's values, the serial continues from the copy's, and the records stay (`tenko_core::dns::ZoneFlip`). `{"to": "secondary", "primaries": [...]}` turns the primary back into a secondary of the named primaries, and the next transfer replaces every record with the copy's. The flip writes one change set per zone and the audit record `dns.zone.flip` with the counts by origin. A catalog zone keeps its own rule and is refused (`dns.zone.flip.catalog`).
2. **The records keep their provenance.** After a flip to primary, the A, AAAA and DHCID RRsets at a name that holds a DHCID RRset, and a PTR whose target is such a name, stay `transfer`: frozen, so that no writer touches a name a DHCP server wrote until the takeover ([dns.md](../dns.md) section 10.7). The apex SOA and NS are replaced. Every other RRset (the controllers' SRV, CNAME and A records, static hosts, the `_msdcs` zone entire) becomes `ad` (WT-Q9, decided 2026-11-05): a record of an AD zone without a known owner, which an update from the controllers' access list may change, as before, and which Slice 13's claim rule governs later ([identity-plan.md](../plans/identity-plan.md) section 3.6). The reverse zones are flipped after the forward zones, so that the PTR rule reads the forward names; a PTR whose target is in a zone Tenko does not hold becomes `ad`.
3. **The frozen names a takeover does not adopt are released by hand.** `POST /dns/zones/{id}/release` turns every remaining `transfer` RRset of a primary zone into `manual`, or deletes them with `delete`, after listing them in a dry run (`tenko_core::dns::ZoneRelease`); audited as `dns.zone.release`. Windows would have scavenged these names; Tenko cannot until DNS-29.
4. **The flip is interactive.** It changes which server is the authority of a zone, so it needs a session: a personal token is refused after the scope check, as the OpenAPI document says.

### The takeover and the adoption rule

5. **The takeover is a job per scope**, started from the mirror's scope row, with the steps of plan section 2.7: the checks (the mirror fresh within 120 s, the scope's losses acknowledged, the prepare's diff empty for pools, exclusions and reservations, Tenko's scope disabled, the forward and reverse zones primary zones on Tenko that take DHCP updates under the profile); a task for the person (the Windows scope deactivated, proven by a push that shows it inactive, waited for at most ten minutes); the records; the leases in batches of 1,000; the scope enabled; the record in `migrate.takeovers` (`tenko_core::migrate::Takeover`: the scope, the sequence, the counts, the adopted addresses and names, the frozen names, the skipped items with their codes `migrate.skip.*`, the before-images of every RRset it changed) and the audit record `dhcp.scope.takeover`. A failure in the records, the leases or the enabling rolls the job back from the before-images. Nothing writes to Windows (CONN-12 explicitly not); the operator's two PowerShell lines per scope are the whole Windows side. The reverse action disables the scope and leaves leases and records (`dhcp.scope.revert`).
6. **Leases.** Each mirrored lease of the scope in the state active or active reservation becomes a `dhcp.leases` row: state `active`, `ends` = `taken_at` plus the remaining time of the Windows lease (at most the Tenko scope's maximum lease time), `granted` = the adoption time ([ADR 0031](0031-the-lease-table-is-the-observation-source.md) point 3), the host name, and the cold field `adopted { mirror, sequence, guessed }` (`tenko_core::dhcp::Adopted`; a serde default and bit 13 of the presence mask of the role's row, so the data format does not move for it). A declined lease becomes an `abandoned` row with the hold of `[dhcp.decline]`. A lease is skipped and reported when its address is outside the Tenko pool and not a reservation, when it conflicts with a Tenko reservation of another client, or when Tenko holds an active row of another client at the address.
7. **The client's identity.** An identifier longer than six octets is the client's option 61 as it is; else, where a DHCID stands at the lease's name, Tenko computes the DHCID of both candidates (type 1 over `01` and the MAC address, type 0 over `htype` 1 and the MAC address) and takes the one that equals the record Windows wrote; else option 61 of type 1 with the MAC address, and the row is marked `guessed`. The fixture `crates/tenko-dns/tests/fixtures/ad-zones/` carries names of both types so that the choice is tested both ways; which type Windows uses is what the rehearsal records (R137).
8. **The engine's rule for an adopted row.** When a REQUEST in RENEWING, REBINDING or INIT-REBOOT names an address whose active row is `adopted` and keyed by another identity, and the row's MAC address equals the request's `chaddr` with `htype` 1, the row is the client's: the change rewrites it under the identity the client presented (a renewal, `granted` kept, `adopted` cleared) instead of refusing the address as another client's, and INIT-REBOOT counts the row as the client's record. A request from another MAC address gets what it gets today: NAK on an authoritative scope, silence otherwise. The check reads fields the row already holds, so the path stays free of allocations. The first REQUEST of every adopted client clears `adopted`, whatever its key (`LeaseChange::to_lease` writes the row without it).
9. **Records.** For each adopted lease and each mirrored reservation with a lease, the name is the lease's host name under the name rule (point 10) and the zone is the scope's forward zone. At that name the takeover adopts the A (or AAAA) and DHCID RRsets of origin `transfer`, and of origin `dynamic` (WT-Q8, decided 2026-11-05: a name Windows DHCP wrote after the flip or during a reverted takeover carries a DHCID and the lease's address, the same proof), when the A RRset holds the lease's address and no other and a DHCID RRset stands beside it: written as A with the lease's address and DHCID computed by Tenko for the row's identity, origin `dhcp`, TTL by the scope's DDNS settings; and the PTR at the address's reverse name whose target is the name, origin `dhcp`. `manual`, `ipam`, `ad` and `dnssec` RRsets, a CNAME, an A RRset with other addresses and a name without a DHCID are never adopted: skipped and reported, and Tenko's DDNS treats the name by its usual rules at the next renewal. The adopted lease row carries what a DDNS write would have left on it (the name as its option 81 name with the S flag, the `ddns` block), so the DDNS worker finds the names to be the lease's own and writes nothing; where the engine rewrote a `guessed` row under another identity, the worker replaces the A and DHCID of the row's own name in one change set and only then forgets the old identity. Names with a DHCID that no mirrored lease or reservation explains stay frozen and are listed in the takeover's record for the `release` action.
10. **Names** (WT-Q4; WT-Q10 decided 2026-11-05). A fully qualified name is kept, lowercased: under a zone Tenko serves it is the record name; outside, it becomes the IPAM host's name and the reservation gets no adopted record, with the loss entry `migrate.loss.foreign_name`. A bare host name gets the scope's forward zone. A factory name is kept as it is: when it is a valid host-name label (`ESP_3A1F2B`, `android-5f2c9e01d4a6b7c8`) it gets the zone like any bare name; when it is not (a space, an apostrophe) the exporter carries it as `IrReservation.source_name`, the prepare keeps it as the IPAM host's name, derives no record and writes `migrate.loss.reservation_name`. A reservation's IPAM address gets no `dns_name`, so its records stay DDNS records with the origin `dhcp`, as Windows DHCP wrote them.
11. **After the flip, the controllers hold secondary copies** (WT-Q12, decided 2026-11-05): the DNS Server role stays on the controllers as secondaries of Tenko's zones, the clients' second DNS server, until a second Tenko node exists (Phase 5). The DNS rollback is then a conversion with current data; Tenko's SOA `expire` for the AD zones is 14 days so the copies outlive an outage (R143).

## Consequences

- Every name stays served through the whole switch: the flip keeps the records, the frozen names wait for the takeover, the takeover adopts them with Tenko's DHCID, and `release` handles what nothing explains. Step 10's gap is closed, for the names Windows wrote in the window too (WT-Q8).
- Each step is reversible in two steps per phase (plan section 2.10): the reverse flip brings the records back as `transfer` at the next transfer; the revert disables the scope and keeps leases and records.
- The engine gains one rule on a path that must stay allocation-free (the counting-allocator test of WP14.2 binds it); a wrong guess of a client's identity is harmless for the same MAC address (R137), and a forged MAC address gets no more than any DHCP client on the network gets today (R147; `ad.dhcid_takeover` tests another MAC address).
- The `Ad` origin, set by nothing until now, is set by the flip in the meaning the identity plan gives it; `dns.md` section 1 changes with WP14.2.
- The frozen names between the flip and the takeover go stale if the window is split (R149): the runbook does both in one window, and `release` handles the rest.
- New codes `dns.zone.flip.*`, `migrate.takeover.*` and `migrate.skip.*`, audit actions `dns.zone.flip`, `dns.zone.release`, `dhcp.scope.prepare`, `dhcp.scope.takeover` and `dhcp.scope.revert`; the lease item of the API gains `adopted`.

## Alternatives considered

- **Today's step 10: delete the Windows-written records and let the renewals write them anew**: leaves every client without a name until it renews. Rejected (WT-Q3).
- **Adopt at the first renewal, when the client proves its identity**: needs no mirror for the records but trusts a renewal from whoever asks first, and a client that never renews keeps a frozen name. Rejected (WT-Q3).
- **Keep `transfer` on every RRset after the flip**: refuses Netlogon's hourly registration of every locator record (event 5774 every hour, a changed controller address never registered). **Make them `manual`**: updatable, but loses the mark that the record came from AD, which Slice 13's claim rule needs. Rejected (WT-Q9).
- **Adopt `transfer` names only**: a name Windows DHCP wrote after the flip is `dynamic` and is refused at the first renewal, which brings step 10's gap back for those names. **Also adopt `manual` names with a DHCID**: would let a takeover change records an operator made. Rejected (WT-Q8).
- **Tidy an invalid factory name (`ESP_3A1F2B` to `esp-3a1f2b`)**: invents a name nobody chose, and the client keeps registering its own under DDNS anyway. **Recognize factory names by their pattern**: guesses at patterns that change by vendor. Rejected (WT-Q10).
- **Remove the DNS Server role from the controllers after the parallel period**: leaves one Tenko node as the only server of the AD zones until Phase 5. **Keep the AD-integrated zones frozen as today**: a stale copy that grows staler. Rejected (WT-Q12).
- **Rewrite every name under the zone as a bare label, or leave the reservations' names out**: rewrites names the operator chose, or loses thirty names the operator typed once. Rejected (WT-Q4).
