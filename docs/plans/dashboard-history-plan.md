# Dashboard history plan: the tiles remember the last days

- Status: Draft, waiting for the owner's decisions (DH-Q1 to DH-Q6 of section 8). No package starts before the owner has answered them
- Date: 2026-11-11
- Scope: OPS-20 (the built-in dashboard) within the cost budget of OPS-21. Each metric tile keeps a history of its figure on a time axis that compresses toward the past: the last minute second by second at the right end, the last hour minute by minute, then the last days hour by hour, with the grid and the labels making the scale visible. Written against `main` at `af974ca4`. Requirement IDs refer to [REQUIREMENTS.md](../REQUIREMENTS.md). Exit criteria, measurements and risks continue the project's series from E114, M81 and R191 (the discovery plan ends at E113, M80 and R190); the slice number is the lead's, and this plan calls it Slice 18 with packages WP18.0 to WP18.4
- Mockup: [`design/mockups/metric-tile-history.html`](../../design/mockups/metric-tile-history.html), opened from the file system or through any static server of the `design/` folder

Terms used below, explained once. A **tier** is a ring of fixed size holding the history at one resolution (60 values of one second, 60 of one minute, and so on); a **bucket** is one slot of a tier, covering one step of time. A bucket keeps **aggregates**: its lowest value (minimum), its highest (maximum), the sum of its values and the time it had a value, from which the **mean** follows. The **band** is the area drawn between the lowest and the highest of each bucket; an **envelope** is a band known to contain the true range without being exactly it; a **lower bound** is a number the true value is at least. **SSE** (server-sent events) is the event stream the UI already listens to; **CBOR** is the compact binary encoding the controllers already use for samples between them; **OpenAPI** is the generated description of the HTTP API. A **controller** is a node that runs the replicated store; the **leader** is the controller that currently coordinates the cluster, and the **term** counts leader changes. A **hot path** is code that runs per DNS query or per DHCP packet; **DORA** (discover, offer, request, acknowledge) is the DHCP exchange that leases an address. **p50** and **p99** are the 50th and 99th percentiles (a **quantile**): the time under which half, or 99 in 100, of the requests finished. **RCODE** is the response code of a DNS answer; **NOERROR** is the code of a normal answer. **SI suffixes** are K, M and G for thousand, million and billion. **KiB** is 1,024 bytes. **UTC** is the time zone-free reference clock. `f32`, `f64` and `u32` are a 4-byte and an 8-byte floating-point number and a 4-byte whole number. **musl** is the C library of the static Linux release binary. A **fuzz target** feeds random input to a parser to find crashes; a **property test** checks a rule against many generated inputs. For people who use a screen reader: a **slider** is the accessibility role of a control whose value moves with the arrow keys; **forced-colors mode** is the high-contrast mode of the operating system, which replaces a page's colors with a few system colors.

## 1. What is built

1. **Tiers in the sampler.** Each controller keeps, for every figure its tiles show, the tiers of DH-Q2 (recommended: 60 × 1 s, 60 × 1 min, 48 × 1 h, two days). The one-second sampler that builds today's sample folds each new value into the open bucket of every tier; nothing in the DNS or DHCP paths changes.
2. **History over the existing stream.** A client that subscribes to the topic `metrics` first receives one event `metrics.history` with every tier of every figure, already combined for the cluster, then the per-second `metrics.snapshot` as today, which now also carries the newest closed buckets.
3. **The tile.** The sparkline becomes the history chart of the mockup: band and mean, the axis design of DH-Q1 with its grid and age labels, hatching where there is no data, a mark where a controller restarted, a readout under the pointer and the keyboard, a text alternative. The tile grows by 50 px (the chart area goes from 28 px to a 64 px plot and a 14 px row of labels, with the list under it), so that a tile with a list (the controllers, the response codes, the busiest lists) keeps a readable chart. The two ratio tiles (cache hit ratio, answers) lose their bar chart of 5-second buckets to the same history chart; their number stays the share of the last 30 seconds.

What stays: the figures, the sample's fields, the cluster merge rule of `docs/observability.md` section 7, the start page's five-minute queries chart, the SI suffixes and the rule that no chart is drawn without its window and its scale. Prometheus at `/metrics` remains the long-term record; this history is a view, not a store. The history is of what the tiles show: the cluster's combination (or the one controller). A history per controller is not kept for display; it exists only as the input of the combination.

## 2. The data model

**Buckets.** One bucket is 32 bytes: lowest and highest as `f32`, the sum as `f64`, a second sum as `f64` for the ratio figures (the hit ratio and the NOERROR share keep numerator and denominator, so a combined bucket is recomputed from counts and never averages percentages), and the milliseconds covered as `u32`. The sampler's tick is not aligned to whole seconds and its rates are means over the sample's `interval_ms` (about 1,000, sometimes more), so a sample is filed under the second in which its interval ends, its value is weighted by `interval_ms`, and the covered time is the sum of the intervals; a second that got no sample is not a gap when the next interval covers it. Bucket edges are fixed on the controller's clock in UTC (a minute bucket starts at a whole minute, an hour bucket at a whole hour), as the five-second buckets of the ratio tiles are today; in a time zone that is not a whole number of hours from UTC (India, parts of Australia) an hour bucket reads as 09:30 to 10:30, and the readout says the span as it is. The ratio figures keep 5 seconds as their finest step (12 buckets for the last minute): a share over one second at a few queries per second jumps between 0, 50 and 100 % and would fill the chart with noise, which is why today's ratio tiles use 5-second buckets.

**Sizes.** The tiles show 25 figures today: the controller's 8, the host's 3, DNS 8 (with the resolver's 2) and DHCP 6. A controller allocates the tiers of the figures its roles produce when the sampler starts, once. Each zone of the axis starts and ends at a round age (1 min, 1 h, 1 d, 2 d, 7 d), and a ring holds every bucket that overlaps its zone, the oldest one partly.

| Reach (DH-Q2) | Tiers | Buckets per figure | Per figure | Each controller, 25 figures | The serving controller of a cluster of 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 h | 60 × 1 s, 60 × 1 min | 120 | 3.8 KiB | 94 KiB | 94 KiB + 2 × 94 KiB of the peers + the combined history and its encoded frame (about 160 KiB) ≈ 440 KiB |
| 2 d (recommended) | 60 × 1 s, 60 × 1 min, 48 × 1 h | 168 | 5.3 KiB | 131 KiB | ≈ 620 KiB |
| 7 d | 60 × 1 s, 60 × 1 min, 24 × 1 h, 25 × 6 h | 169 | 5.3 KiB | 132 KiB | ≈ 625 KiB |

**Who fills them.** The `Sampler` of `crates/tenko-api/src/sse/metrics.rs` gets a `History` beside it (a new module `sse/metrics/history.rs`): after building the sample of the tick, `History::fold` adds each figure's value to the open bucket of each tier, and when a minute or an hour ends it closes the bucket and moves the ring's head. The rings are allocated once, so the fold allocates nothing. Today the publisher sleeps while nobody listens; the history needs the sampler's tick to run always. What runs always is the local part: the registry snapshot, the sample and the fold. That part allocates as it does today (the snapshot, the sample's lists and its encoding build small vectors once a second), which OPS-21 allows off the hot path. The calls to the other controllers and the publishing still run only while a stream listens. While the tick runs always, the answer to a peer's `Metrics.Snapshot` is the last tick's sample and never a sample taken on demand, so a peer's call does not reset the baseline of the local rates.

**One history per controller, encoded once.** The controller a client talks to keeps one combined history of the cluster, updated as buckets close, and encodes it once per change. Every new subscriber gets that cached frame as its first event; nothing is built per subscription, in keeping with the rule that a frame is encoded once and shared (`docs/observability.md` section 7). This matters for security first: any account that may read the metrics can open many streams, and per-subscription work would let it multiply the controller's load. Sending a first event of its own to one subscriber is new for the stream: today a stream gets the hello, the replay and the broadcast frames, so `sse/hub.rs` and `sse/mod.rs` gain a per-topic first frame. The cached frame is about 100 KB of JSON with the recommended reach (25 figures, 168 buckets, five numbers each).

**What crosses the event stream.** The first event of a subscription to `metrics` is `metrics.history`: for each figure the tiers as arrays of the lowest, highest and mean values (the ratio figures as their recomputed share) and the time covered, the start time of each tier's newest bucket, the time each controller started, and per bucket how many controllers it covers. A client that connects late, reconnects or opens a second page therefore draws every tile at once, without a request per tile. Each later `metrics.snapshot` carries `closed`: for each tier, the newest two closed buckets with their start times. A bucket arrives twice and the client keeps it once by its start, so a missed second loses nothing; a client that finds a hole in the starts, or receives the stream's `reset` frame (sent to a client too slow to keep up), subscribes again and gets a fresh `metrics.history`. The client only appends and never aggregates. The event has no id and is never replayed, as today. The topic's permission (the read of the metrics object) admits both events.

**Between controllers.** A new call `Metrics.History` (CBOR, on the cluster port, with the certificate and role checks of `Metrics.Snapshot`) returns a controller's tiers. The serving controller calls it when a peer first appears and when a peer answers again after missing calls, not per subscription; from then on it takes the peer's closed buckets from the same `closed` field in the peer's answer to the per-second `Metrics.Snapshot`, deduplicated by start. A peer of the previous release answers `UNIMPLEMENTED` and sends no `closed`: its buckets are missing from the combination, which is drawn and worded like a controller that is down ("2 of 3 controllers reported").

## 3. Restart, leader change, an unreachable controller, and gaps

**Restart (DH-Q3).** With history in memory only, a restarted controller starts with empty tiers. On one controller the tile shows hatching before the restart, a mark at the restart, and says in the readout and the text alternative "No history before 07:21, when this controller restarted". In a cluster the buckets before the restart lack that controller: they are drawn with a lighter band and a bar under the baseline and say "2 of 3 controllers reported". Whatever happened before the restart on that controller is gone from the tile, a burst included (the mockup's "Cluster of 3" with "Memory only" shows it); the logs and Prometheus keep it. With the small file of DH-Q3 (b), only the downtime is missing after a clean stop; after a crash, the time since the last write (up to an hour) is missing too.

**Leader change.** Nothing happens to the history: the tiers belong to each controller, not to the leader, and any controller can combine them. The Raft tile shows the new term.

**The client's controller goes away.** The UI reconnects (to the same address, or to another controller behind a proxy) and the new subscription brings that controller's cached `metrics.history`.

**An unreachable controller.** It goes on filling its own tiers. While it does not answer, the combined buckets lack it and say "2 of 3 controllers". When it answers again, the serving controller fetches its tiers once more, rebuilds the combined history and sends the new frame to the streams that are open, so the past heals; only the time the controller itself was down stays missing.

**Clock.** A clock that jumps back never rewrites a closed bucket (the values go into the open one); a jump forward leaves empty buckets. The combination matches buckets by their start; two controllers whose clocks differ by more than a second blur the one-second tier by that much (R192).

**Drawing a gap.** A bucket without any data draws no band and no line: the time is hatched with sparse lines in `--chart-ink` on the surface, the way the address map hatches what is not in use. A bucket with some time missing is drawn as usual and its readout says "41 s of 1 min". A restart is a hairline in `--ink-3` with a small mark at the top. A bucket that not every controller covers has a lighter band and a 2 px bar in `--chart-ink` under the baseline.

## 4. The cluster's tiles

A sum of maxima is not the maximum of the sum: the busiest second of controller 1 and that of controller 2 are rarely the same second. The combination therefore follows the existing per-field rule and says what each number is:

| Figure kind (merge rule of section 7) | Mean | Lowest and highest | What the readout says |
|---|---|---|---|
| Summed (rates, sizes, counts) | Sum of the controllers' means: exact where every controller covered the whole bucket | Sum of the lowests and sum of the highests: an envelope that contains the true range | "The mean is exact; the true range lies inside" |
| Highest of the controllers (quantiles, the lag, the host's CPU) | Highest mean: a lower bound of the mean of the per-second highest | Highest of the highests (exact), highest of the lowests (a lower bound) | "The highest is exact; the mean and the lowest are lower bounds" |
| Least available (the host's memory and disk) | The controller with the smallest share available in that bucket: its own mean | That controller's own lowest and highest | "The controller with the smallest share available" |
| Ratio (hit ratio, NOERROR share) | Recomputed from the summed counts: exact | Lowest of the lowests, highest of the highests: an envelope | "Recomputed from the counts" |

The host's memory and disk follow the controller with the smallest share available today (`sample/host.rs`). The history keeps each controller's available bytes and its total, and the combined bucket takes the controller whose mean share available is the smallest in that bucket, so it follows the same rule as the live figure; the Disk tile draws that controller's available GiB. The one-second tier of a cluster combines one sample per controller per second and is exact up to the clocks' difference (R192). DH-Q5 asks whether the envelope is good enough or whether the cluster should keep exact tiers of its own.

## 6. Accessibility

**Readable without the chart.** Each chart has a text alternative linked to it (`aria-describedby`), refreshed every five seconds, in the form of the mockup: "DNS queries: 752 per second now. Last minute: 690 to 810. Last hour: mean 740, highest 1.1K at 14:42. Last 2 days: highest 9.8K between 09:00 and 10:00, lowest 210. No history before 07:21, when this controller restarted." Each tier is scanned at its own step, so a peak in a 6-hour bucket is named with its 6-hour span. A figure that never moved says "0 throughout".

**Keyboard and screen readers.** The chart is one tab stop with the role `slider`: its value is the bucket, its value text is the readout, so a screen reader speaks the bucket at each step and passes the arrow keys to the chart (a plain group would leave them to the reader's own browsing keys). Left and Right move one bucket, with Shift ten, Page Up and Page Down jump to the next tier, Home and End go to the oldest and the newest, and Escape closes the readout and keeps the focus; the name of the chart lists these keys. The readout under the pointer and under the keyboard is the same element. While the pointer or the focus is on a chart, the chart holds still ("Held") so the bucket under it does not move; the number above it keeps updating. A tap on a touch screen reads the tapped bucket.

**Contrast and color.** Every mark that carries data reaches 3:1 against both surfaces, the minimum for graphics: the mean in `--series-1` at 1.5 px (4.4:1 light, 4.8:1 dark), the band's upper edge in full `--series-1` at 1 px, which is what shows a burst of five seconds in an hour bucket whose mean barely moves, the hatch of no data and the bar of partial coverage in `--chart-ink`, the restart in `--ink-3`. The band itself is a wash of `--series-1` and carries nothing the edge and the readout do not. The accent marks only the bucket under the pointer and the focus. Nothing depends on color alone: no data is a hatch and words, a restart a line and words, partial coverage a bar and words. Today's sparkline draws in `--accent`; the history chart moves the tiles to `--series-1`, as the DNS panel's chart, because the design language keeps the accent for selection and focus. In forced-colors mode the mean, the upper edge, the restart and the marks take `CanvasText`, the band and the zone fill are dropped, and the bucket under the pointer takes `Highlight`.

**Motion.** Nothing animates: the chart is redrawn in place once per second. Under reduced motion it is redrawn once every five seconds.

## 8. Owner decisions

Each question gives the options, where they stand in the owner's order (security, performance, usability, features) and one recommendation.

**DH-Q1. The axis.** (a) Three zones, each linear in time, set off by a stronger line and a quiet fill; (b) one continuous logarithmic axis with a knee at 10 s. Security and performance: equal. Usability: (a) keeps time even inside a zone, so slopes compare there and the eye learns where "an hour ago" is; (b) has no boundary and looks closer to the memory graph, but its buckets get narrower inside a tier, so slopes and widths deceive. Features: equal. **Recommendation: (a).**

**DH-Q2. How far back.** (a) 1 hour (120 buckets, 94 KiB per controller); (b) 2 days (168 buckets, 131 KiB); (c) 7 days (169 buckets with a 6-hour tier, 132 KiB). Security: (c) keeps an attack in view longest, (a) loses a night's events by morning. Performance: equal within 40 KiB. Usability: (b) shows today's rhythm against yesterday's at hour resolution; (c) squeezes the hours into a narrow zone. **Recommendation: (b).**

**DH-Q3. Across a restart.** (a) Memory only; (b) a small file per controller (`metrics-history.bin` in the data directory, 131 KiB, written atomically at each hour's end and at a clean stop, about 3 MiB of writes a day, read once at start, refused and started empty on any mismatch). Security: (a) adds nothing; (b) adds a parser of a file the service account writes (no privilege is crossed, but it needs a fuzz target and a strict format), and an update no longer erases the picture of an attack. Performance: (b) costs one small write an hour. Usability: (b) keeps the history over every update, which the owner installs weekly. **Recommendation: (a) for the first release, with (b) as a follow-up package if the restarts of updates turn out to wipe what the owner wants to see.**

**DH-Q4. Which tiles first.** (a) All 25 at once; (b) DNS and host first, the rest in a second step. Security: the DNS tiles show attacks, and both options include them. Performance: equal. Usability: one form on the whole page reads better than two. **Recommendation: (a): the work per tile is the same chart, and the contracts change once.**

**DH-Q5. The cluster's band.** (a) The envelope of section 4, combined from each controller's own tiers; (b) exact tiers of the cluster, which the serving controller would keep from the combined sample of each second; that needs the calls between controllers every second even when nobody watches, and the tiers are lost whenever the controller a client talks to changes. Security and performance: (a) adds no traffic between controllers; (b) adds one call per peer per second for good. Usability: (b) is exact; (a) says what it shows. **Recommendation: (a).**

**DH-Q6. The scale.** (a) The highest value in view; (b) the highest after holding each lone needle to half again its neighbors (two buckets on each side), with a needle above the scale drawn to the top with a caret and its value in the readout and the text alternative. Security: both keep a burst visible; (b) also keeps the normal rhythm readable beside it. Usability: under (a) one burst of 9.8K flattens a day of 1K into the baseline. **Recommendation: (b).**

## 10. Risks

| # | Risk | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| R191 | The always-on tick costs an idle home node more than expected | M81 gates the tick; the fold is a fixed number of additions; the peer calls stay listener-only |
| R192 | Controllers' clocks differ, so the combined one-second tier blurs | Time synchronization is a dependency (OPS-09); buckets match by start; WP18.1 compares each peer's sample time with its own and, above 2 s, names that controller in the readout |
| R193 | A reader takes the envelope for the true range of the cluster | The readout and the text alternative say what the band is (section 4) |
| R194 | Memory-only history hides an attack that happened before an update's restart | Said in the readout; Prometheus and the logs remain the record; DH-Q3 (b) is the remedy |
| R195 | Many streams opened by one account multiply the history's cost | One cached frame per controller, built per change and not per subscription (section 2); E115 opens a thousand streams |
